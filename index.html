Elpis Fallen Guide

A player resource for Elpis: Fallen Star

Tier List

This is a general tier list for most content, with more weight given to end-game content such as Formidable Assault and Illusory Realm (weekly boss) performance.

Apex characters are very strong in almost all teams and content. These are the best characters in each class.

T0 characters are very strong in specific teams and content, and are good in all teams and content.

T1 characters can be very strong in specific teams or content, but otherwise not as good as T0 and Apex units.

T2 characters require specific teams or content to be strong, are good for filling out your team when starting the game, or building a second or third team.

Elpis: Fallen Star is a game where the majority of content can be completed with the majority of characters, as long as you are building your teams correctly. A roster full of Apex and T0 characters is only required for those who wish to be competitive in high ranking PVP, Formidable Assault, Illusory Realm, and clear the last stage of events.

For example, I sometimes use Haidee, a 2-star voidblade that doesn't even show up on this tier list. Why do I use her then? For some events, having another lumin type voidblade that reduces enemy defense can be useful. Sometimes I use her in a 3rd team on a boss when multiple teams can be deployed.

5-star units are evaluated at awakening 0 (no duplicates), and all other units are evaluated at max awakening.

This tier list represents my own experience with these units, comparisons with other tier lists (linked below), and discussions with others.

Team Building Guide

Basics of Team Building

  • A team consists of 8 units
  • You usually want a mix of all classes:
    • Ironclad: Frontline defender/tank
    • Voidblade: Frontline damage dealer or support
    • Spellweaver: Midrange damage dealer, support, or healer
    • Ghosthunter: Backline damage dealer or support
  • The standard team structure for most content is 2 of each class (2-2-2-2)
  1. The team needs to deal damage and survive enemy attacks. Thus we need damage dealers, support units, defenders, and healers.
  2. Total Assault phase and skill cooldown timing.

Q: What is the Total Assault (TA) phase?

A: During battle, your Total Assault meter will fill over time. Once full, you can execute the TA phase. When this phase begins, you can select units to perform their TA skill (i.e., their ultimate attack). One of each class may be selected during a single TA phase. If you have eight ghosthunter class units, then only one unit gets to use their TA skill. If you have one of each class, then four units get to use their TA skill. Additionally, TA skills have cooldowns ranging from 20 seconds to 70 seconds. A cooldown of 45+ seconds means that this unit can't use their TA skill on the next phase. The 2-2-2-2 team composition means that four units will use their TA skill every total assault phase.

Elemental Advantages

Pay attention to unit and enemy element type, and deploy units with an elemental advantage.

Fire > Grass > Water > Fire
Lumin > Umbra AND Umbra > Lumin

  • For Fire/Grass/Water type advantage, the unit deals 30% extra damage and reduces incoming damage by 30% (the unit takes 70% of the original damage)
  • For Umbra and Lumin types, they will always deal 30% extra damage to the other unit.

Q: If Lumin and Umbra both deal extra damage, what's the advantage?

A: Units do not always fight 1v1, and we can use this to create type mismatches with enemies. Consider the following:

  • Fire type ironclad blocks movement and takes aggro from Umbra type Voidblade.
  • Your lumin type ghosthunter can attack the umbra type enemy and deal extra damage due to the type advantage, while avoiding taking extra damage since a friendly fire type ironclad is taking aggro instead.
  • This strategy is very useful against bosses and strong single target enemies.

Advanced Team Building

2-3-1-2 Team

  • Against some enemies and bosses, Voidblade units can struggle to stay alive.
  • Deploy a voidblade unit with a 20 second cooldown (ex: Lexie), they will use their TA skill every TA phase.
  • We have a few options for the third Spellweaver slot:
    1. A healer that doesn't need to use their TA skill often if at all to keep the team alive
    2. Monna: She always uses her TA skill on odd rounds, and she doesn't need to be selected during the TA phase. This means that she can use her TA skill at the same time as another Spellweaver!
    3. A Spellweaver that has decent DPS from team buffs and other sources without using their TA skill.

3-2-2-1 Team

  • Some battles require maximum DPS and lower frontline tanking. The risk of failure in these fights is due to running out of time or your backrow damage dealers being killed before your tank dies, so having an extra Ironclad doesn't help as much.
  • Options for the ghosthunter units:
    1. Lingyao: Lingyao's TA skill allows you to select a second ghosthunter unit and use their TA in the same round. This means that during round one, Lingyao and Ghosthunter 2 use their TA skill, and then during round two, Ghosthunter 3 uses their TA skill (assuming that Ghosthunter 2 has a cooldown of 45+ seconds).
    2. Delis: Her leader skill provides +15% crit rate and +30 crit damage to your entire team. Her basic attacks also occasionally stun enemies.
    3. Ciel: Same leader skill as Delis (+15% crit rate and +30 crit damage to your entire team).
    4. A Ghosthunter that has decent DPS from team buffs and other sources without using their TA skill.

Unit Synergies

These units synergize very well together and can be useful starting points when building a team.

DoT Team: Souffle + Ethel

Key Units: Souffle, Ethel, Other DoT units (Asteria, Falcon, Absolver, Gima, Timmy, Skylark)

  • Souffle
    DPS DoT
    Souffle's TA skill does extra damage for each stack of DoT, and hits three targets (or the same target three times). To maximize her DPS, we want to apply as much DoT as possible.
  • Ethel's TA skill does extra damage (ignores enemy defense) for each stack of DoT, and hits three targets. It's a 20 second cooldown TA, so you don't need a second Voidblade for this team (unless desired), since you want her using her TA every round.
  • Falcon applies DoT as long as she is shielded, Asteria will buff both Ethel and Souffle TA damage, and she also provides DoT. Timmy, Absolver, Gima, and Skylark can also be good DoT applicators.
  • Notice that many of these units are also Hydro type units, so can be useful against fire element bosses.

Judgement Stack: Inellis + Ivorywing

  • Ivorywing TA skill creates an AoE damage zone around herself and one other nearby unit with a defense buff (Jessica, Kaguya, or other Ironclad or Voidblade)
  • Inellis leader skill boosts Ivorywing TA skill damage. Usually not worth it unless you have Ivorywing at awakening 3+.
  • Ivorywing TA skill applies a stack of judgement to the enemies in the AoE zone.
  • Inellis' combat skill and TA skill do additional damage for each stack of judgement.
  • Make sure Ivorywing uses her TA skill the same round as Inellis for max damage, and that you have another character in the frontline with a defense buff.

Healing Synergy: Ghostsamurai + Freya

  • Ghostsamurai is an amazing Ironclad because in addition to tanking and doing okay damage (for an Ironclad), she:
    1. Creates a shield for the entire team with her TA skill
    2. Heals allied units passively.
  • To trigger her healing as often as possible, you need to apply time stop or stun to an enemy, which is why Freya pairs well with her.
  • Her healing can be strong enough in some fights that you don't need a dedicated healer, and when you need a lot of healing is a great secondary healer since she doesn't take up a Spellweaver spot which is better reserved for a DPS or sub-DPS+buffer/debuffer.

Leticia Ultimate Combo

Key Units: Lingyao, 2 other ghosthunters, Leticia, Eclipse

  • Leticia reduces TA cooldown by 20 seconds, which is enough to enable units with a 45 second cooldown to use TA skill twice in a row.
  • This means that:
    1. Round one: Leticia → Lingyao → 2nd Ghosthunter
    2. Round two: Eclipse → Lingyao → 2nd Ghosthunter
    3. Round three: other units use TA skill
  • In addition to doing huge damage, Leticia also applies a -10% debuff to enemies during her TA skill.
  • Eclipse buffs Ghosthunter damage significantly with her TA skill
  • The end result is that Lingyao and your second best Ghosthunter will get to go 2 out of 3 TA phases, instead of every other TA phase, and they will have significant damage buffs or enemy debuffs for those phases.
  • I usually run the third ghosthunter as Ciel or Delis for their leader skill

Stun/Lock Team

Key Units: Freya, Delis, Solace, Teostra, Jessica

  • Freya applies time stop with her TA skill, and also with basic attacks at A3+ awakening.
  • Delis applies stun with basic attacks occasionally.
  • Teostra applies stun with his TA skill.
  • Jessica stuns enemies with her petrify ability.
  • The Solace provides healing and also an attack speed buff, which makes Freya and Delis stun and apply time stop more frequently.

PVP

To succeed in PVP at a high level, the goal is to survive long enough and rip off your TA skills first so that you kill the enemy before they have a chance to use their TA skills. Since the defending team uses their TA skill first by default, a few units have unique skills that make them specifically good for PVP:

  • Osadin: Increases TA meter at beginning of battle, meaning you get to use your TA skill before the enemy.
  • Jessica: When she takes enough damage, she will petrify (stun) multiple enemies. If the petrify happens at the right time, you will get to use your TA skills first.
  • Darkwing: On her first strike she deals a significant portion of bonus damage. If you build her correctly and line her up to target important backrow characters (such as Lingyao, Inellis, or Ghostqueen) you can reduce the enemy's damage output during their TA skill.
  • Inellis: At high awakening (A3+) or even low awakening with good emblems and buffs, her TA skill is strong enough to take out multiple enemies at once.

Is Kaguya Bad?

Why you shouldn't use the Terra Blessing emblem set with Kaguya

On some tier lists, Kaguya is listed as a T2 or "EX" character despite being a 5-star rarity. Guides also frequently advise new players against selecting her or rerolling their account if they get her early. This has lead some to believe that she is a bad unit.

Kaguya is a strong unit, but she is not a great character at the beginning of the game. Why?

  1. She is pure tank. Her combat skill is passive self healing. Her TA skill makes her stop attacking (does no damage during TA phase), significantly buffs her defense, and also nullifies negative status effects.
  2. Early in the game you don't need this much defense from a single character in most battles. You need healing for team members, which is what makes Ghostsamurai so elite. You also benefit more from units like Aria that apply debuffs or Jessica that buffs your voidblade units.

Once you advance farther into the game, there are many situations where Kaguya makes sense to use over another Ironclad. She is a great candidate for the Crimson Pact, Ruinous Blaze, or Guiding Beacon emblem sets, which increase team member damage. While the game recommends that Kaguya use the Terra Blessing set (which increases health and damage reduction), I find that these benefits are overkill most of the time. For most bosses, Kaguya should use the Crimson Pact set, because she will still have enough defensive capabilities to tank and survive, and it will give all Ghosthunter units on your team a 10% damage boost for the entire battle.

In short: Kaguya is not bad, she just isn't Ghostsamurai. She is situationally good and won't help you in the early game nearly as much as in the end game. If what you need is a front row tank, she does that job perfectly.
